cell shows a phone's cellular service, activates it on a network, and sends and reads messages. A phone is a computer (usually a pocket computer) with a wireless modem. Once activated, it attaches to the strongest cell of its network by itself, and keeps its address and connections while moving between cells.

ex:
"cell" shows the network, the phone's number and address, and the cells it can hear.
"cell monitor" shows the phone's service on one screen, kept up to date: its state, number and address, the cell it uses and how strong it is, every cell it hears (of any network), and what has happened lately, such as moving between cells. A cell marked ">" is the one in use, and "!" one which claims to be of the phone's network but isn't. Press Q to quit. It works before the phone is activated too, to see which networks are around.
"cell activate ACME 7Q2M-K4XD-9PRB-T6WN-HJ3C" activates the phone on the network ACME, with a code from its administrator.
"cell send 0042 Meet at the station" sends a message to the phone with the number 0042.
"cell messages" lists the messages this phone has received and sent.
"cell networks" lists the networks around and the SIM's own: which one is in use (">"), whether the phone may use it (its "home", a "partner" its own network has a roaming agreement with, or "not allowed"), and how strong its nearest cell is.
"cell network OTHERNET" makes the phone use the network OTHERNET only, and "cell network auto" lets it choose again: its own network where it can hear it, and otherwise a partner's (roaming).
"cell off" turns cell service off, and "cell on" turns it on again. While it's off, the phone doesn't listen or send, and has no address. The SIM and messages are kept.
"cell gui" shows menus to read, send and answer messages, to choose a network (or let the phone choose), to turn cell service off and on, and to open the monitor. Choose with Up, Down and Enter or with the mouse, go back with Backspace or Left, and quit with Q. The top line is a status bar: the network's name, an R when roaming, and the signal as four bars, with a note in the middle when there are new messages. There's no bar while cell service is turned off, or before the phone is activated.
"cell bar" runs a shell beneath the same status bar, and "cell bar <program> [arguments]" another program. Put "cell bar" in a startup file to always have the bar.
