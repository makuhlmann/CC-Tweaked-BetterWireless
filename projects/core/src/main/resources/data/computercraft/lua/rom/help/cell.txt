cell shows a phone's cellular service, activates it on a network, and sends and reads messages. A phone is a computer (usually a pocket computer) with a wireless modem. Once activated, it attaches to the strongest cell of its network by itself, and keeps its address and connections while moving between cells.

ex:
"cell" shows the network, the phone's number and address, and the cells it can hear.
"cell monitor" shows the phone's service on one screen, kept up to date: its state, number and address, the cell it uses and how strong it is, every cell it hears (of any network), and what has happened lately, such as moving between cells. A cell marked ">" is the one in use, and "!" one which claims to be of the phone's network but isn't. Press Q or Backspace to quit. It works before the phone is activated too, to see which networks are around.
"cell activate ACME 7Q2M-K4XD-9PRB-T6WN-HJ3C" activates the phone on the network ACME, with a code from its administrator. Where ACME has no cells in range (or none at all), it activates through another network's cell, which passes it on to ACME if it carries ACME's phones.
"cell send 0042 Meet at the station" sends a message to the phone with the number 0042. A phone of another network which exchanges messages with this one has that network's number prefix in front: "cell send 6660042 Hello". Without service (or while cell service is off), the message waits, even if the computer restarts, and is sent once the phone has service.
"cell messages" lists the messages this phone has received and sent.
"cell networks" lists the networks around and the SIM's own: which one is in use (">"), whether the phone may use it (its "home", or a network it treats as home, a "partner" its own network has a roaming agreement with, or "unknown" when it knows of no agreement), and how strong its nearest cell is.
"cell network OTHERNET" makes the phone use the network OTHERNET only (even an unknown one, which it tries), and "cell network auto" lets it choose again: its own network where it can hear it, and otherwise a partner's (roaming). It stays on its own network until it loses it, and goes back once its own network is stronger than the one it roams on. Where it hears neither, it tries other networks too, in case they have an agreement it hasn't heard of yet; one which turns it away is left alone for a minute.
"cell off" turns cell service off, and "cell on" turns it on again. While it's off, the phone doesn't listen or send, and has no address. The SIM and messages are kept.
"cell gui" shows menus to read and write messages, to choose a network (or let the phone choose), to turn cell service off and on, and to open the monitor. Choose with Up, Down and Enter or with the mouse, go back with Q, Backspace or Left, and quit from the first menu with Q or Backspace. Messages are a conversation with each number: the list has the newest first (N writes to another number, D deletes a conversation), and a conversation has the messages from the number on the left and those sent to it on the right, with how each got on, above a line to write in. Enter sends, even without service: the message waits until there is some. There Q is just a letter: Backspace in an empty line goes back. Tab chooses a message instead, to delete it (D) or send one which wasn't sent again (R); Tab, Q or Backspace go back to writing. The top line is a status bar, in dark grey: the network's name, and the signal as four bars in brackets, such as [||..], with an R before them when roaming (R [||..]), and a note in the middle when there are new messages. There's no bar while cell service is turned off, or before the phone is activated.
"cell bar" runs a shell beneath the same status bar, and "cell bar <program> [arguments]" another program. Put "cell bar" in a startup file to always have the bar.
