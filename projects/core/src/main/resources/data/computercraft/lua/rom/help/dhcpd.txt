dhcpd is a DHCP server: it hands out addresses to computers on one of its interfaces, which must have a fixed address, and may register their names at a DNS server (dnsd). Leases are saved in /etc/dhcpd.leases, so computers keep their addresses when the server restarts. It runs in the background, as a service (see "help service"), and starts with the computer. What it hands out is in its log.

Its settings are in /etc/dhcpd.conf, which it writes the first time it starts, for the first interface with a fixed address. Each line is a setting's name, then its values, separated by spaces, and # starts a comment. Changes take effect when it starts again ("dhcpd restart"):
- "interface top" hands out addresses on the interface top. "address 10.0.0.1" uses the interface with that address instead.
- "range 100 199" hands out .100 to .199 of the network (the default). "range 10.0.0.50 10.0.0.80" gives the addresses.
- "lease 3600" makes leases last an hour (30 seconds to a week). Clients renew them halfway through.
- "gateway 10.0.0.1", "dns 10.0.0.1 10.0.0.2" and "domain home" are passed on to clients.
- "update-server 10.0.0.1" registers each client's name (its hostname: the net.hostname setting, or its label) at that DNS server, for as long as its lease lasts, in the zone "update-zone home" (or else the domain). "update-key s3cret" signs the updates, if the server asks for a key. The server must let this computer update names (dnsd.conf's allow-update), unless it's this computer.
- "host 12 10.0.0.50" gives computer 12 the address 10.0.0.50, always, and nobody else. It may be outside the range.

Before, dhcpd took its interface and range as arguments, and the settings dhcpd.lease, dhcpd.dns, dhcpd.domain and dhcpd.gateway. The first time it starts, it moves them to /etc/dhcpd.conf, with its leases, and the settings go.

ex:
"dhcpd start" starts it, from now on.
"dhcpd restart" starts it again, with its settings as they are now.
"dhcpd stop" stops it, and it doesn't start with the computer.
"dhcpd status" says how it's doing, and what it's handed out.
"dhcpd run" hands out addresses in the foreground instead, until Ctrl+T is pressed.
