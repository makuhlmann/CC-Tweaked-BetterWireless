Sockets from net.udp.open have the methods send( target, port, data ), receive( [timeout] ), getPort() and close(). Datagrams may be lost.

Connections deliver messages in order and exactly once. They have the methods send( value ), receive( [timeout] ), close(), abort(), getPeer(), getPort(), getId() and isOpen(). Listeners from net.tcp.listen have accept( [timeout] ), close() and getPort(). Streams from net.stream read and write bytes over a connection, like a file.

Secure connections from net.secure are encrypted, and both sides know who they're talking to. They have the same methods, and getPeerKey(), getPeerName() and getTrust(). A changed, replayed or cut short message ends the connection. Datagrams and plain connections aren't encrypted.

The network stack runs in the background on every computer. Every modem is a network interface. Interfaces get addresses from a DHCP server, or give themselves link-local ones, and computers answer to <hostname>.local. Routers forward packets between networks. See also the programs "ifconfig", "ping", "traceroute", "route", "firewall", "nslookup", "netdump", "nc", "ncp", "rsh", "rshd", "netkeys", "cell", "celld", "cellcore", "dhcpd" and "dnsd".
